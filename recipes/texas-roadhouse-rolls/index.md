# Texas Roadhouse Rolls

The impossibly soft steakhouse rolls, at home, by the basket.

Prep: 25 min (plus 1.5 hr rising) · Cook: 15 min · Makes: 24 rolls

## Ingredients

- 1 cup (240ml) whole milk, warmed to 105-110°F
- 2 1/4 tsp (1 packet) active dry yeast
- 3 Tbsp honey
- 55g (4 Tbsp) unsalted butter, melted and cooled, plus more for brushing
- 1 large egg
- 480g (4 cups) all-purpose flour
- 1 1/2 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt (3/4 tsp table salt)

## Steps

1. Stir warm milk, yeast, and 1 Tbsp of the honey in a large bowl. Let sit 5-10 minutes until foamy. No foam = dead yeast; start over rather than bake bricks.
2. Whisk in remaining honey, melted butter, and the egg. Add flour and salt and mix into a shaggy dough.
3. Knead 8-10 minutes by hand (or 5-6 in a stand mixer with the dough hook) until smooth and slightly tacky but not sticking to clean hands. Add flour 1 Tbsp at a time only if it's gluing itself to everything.
4. Place in a greased bowl, cover, and rise in a warm spot until doubled, about 1 hour.
5. Punch down. On a lightly floured surface, roll into a rectangle about 1/2 inch thick. Fold it in half like a book and gently roll to seal - this fold is what makes the signature tall, layered square.
6. Cut into 24 squares with a sharp knife or bench scraper. Place on two parchment-lined sheet pans, 1 inch apart. Cover and rise 30-45 minutes until puffy.
7. Bake at 350°F (175°C) for 12-15 minutes until deep golden on top. Rotate pans halfway.
8. Brush immediately and generously with melted butter. Serve warm, with the cinnamon honey butter.

## Notes

- The fold-then-cut trick is the whole Texas Roadhouse look - skip it and you have good dinner rolls with the wrong soul.
- Weigh the flour. 4 scooped cups can be 5 actual cups, and that's a dense roll.
- Freeze baked rolls up to 3 months; refresh at 300°F for 8 minutes and butter again.

## When it goes wrong

- Dense, heavy rolls → too much flour or a cold rise; weigh it, and give the dough a warm spot.
- Didn't rise → yeast was dead or the milk was hot enough to kill it; 105-110°F, foamy proof, no shortcuts.
- Pale tops → underbaked or oven runs cool; look for deep golden, not blond.
- Tough crumb → over-kneaded after adding too much flour; tacky is correct.
